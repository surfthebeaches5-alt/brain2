What's
Actually In
Your Control

The Stoic Principle

One of the core stoic principles is the Dichotomy of Control. What this means? Epictetus summed it up pretty well:

"Some things are up to us, and some things are not."

This might sound obvious, but it's actually revolutionary. The Stoics figured out that a lot of our suffering comes from trying to control things that aren't ours to control.

Here's the simple split:

Your Domain

(What you actually control):

  • Your thoughts and how you talk to yourself
  • Your beliefs and values
  • Your actions and responses
  • How much effort you put in
  • What you choose to focus on

Not Your Domain

(What you don't control):

  • Other people's choices, feelings, and opinions
  • Outcomes and results
  • The past (it's done)
  • The future (it's not here yet)
  • External events and circumstances
The Ship Metaphor:

You're the captain of your ship, not the weather.

You can't control the storms, the wind, or the waves. But you can control how you navigate through them. You can adjust your sails, change course, and decide how to respond to whatever conditions you face.

Why This Feels So
Damn Hard (Modern
Psychology Insight)

Your Brain's Control Addiction

If this concept feels impossible to accept… I get it. I've been there. And guess what? It's totally human!

Here's why your mind fights this wisdom:

1) Powerlessness Feels Dangerous:

Your brain literally hates accepting powerlessness because this triggers your survival system. Feeling out of control makes your nervous system think you're in danger, even when you're just worried about what another person thinks of something you did.

2) Threat Detection Mode:

When you feel anxious, your mind desperately grabs for control as a way to feel safe. This is why you might find yourself:

  • Doom-scrolling news you can't influence
  • Checking your phone obsessively
  • Replaying conversations to find the "right" thing you should have said
  • Planning for every possible scenario

3) CBT Connection – Locus of Control:

Psychology research shows that people with an "internal locus of control" (believing they have power over their lives) are generally happier and more resilient than people with an external locus of control (believing that outcomes in life are determined by factors outside of one's own control, such as luck, fate, or other people).

But here's the key: it's about controlling the right things (aka your responses, not the world around you).

Gentle reminder

Trying to control things you can't is the fastest path to burnout. Acceptance isn't giving up; it's growing up.

The Soft Stoicism
Approach:
Let Go, Gently

Not Cold Detachment, But Warm Release

Remember that in our soft stoicism approach, we don't aim to becoming an emotionless robot. Rather, we want to release our grip on things that were never ours to hold in the first place… but doing it with self-compassion.

Here are some suggestions on how to achieve that:

Reframing Control as Careful Energy Investment

Instead of asking "How do I control this?" ask:

  • "Where is my energy best spent?"
  • "Am I trying to influence this because I'm scared, or because it's truly my responsibility?"
  • "What's the most loving thing I can do for myself here?"

Language Swaps That Change Everything

×Old Pattern:"How do I fix this?"
+New Pattern:"What can I influence here?"
×Old Pattern:"I need them to understand me."
+New Pattern:"I can only speak my truth clearly and kindly."
×Old Pattern:"This shouldn't be happening."
+New Pattern:"This is what's happening. How do I want to respond?"
×Old Pattern:"They made me feel…"
+New Pattern:"I'm feeling… in response to…"

The Pattern You'll Notice

You can't control outcomes, but you can always control your input.

You can't control other people's choices, but you can control your own response.

Note that this isn't about being passive! When we focus on what we control, we're still very much active, only now we're more strategic with our energy.

Practicing
The Shift

Mini Mantras for Releasing Control

Here are some quick mindset resets (perfect for sticky notes or phone backgrounds!):

  • "I don't control outcomes, I control effort."
  • "Let it be messy. Let it be enough."
  • "My peace is not dependent on their response."
  • "Feel the urge to fix, and pause anyway."
  • "I can care without carrying." (see what I did there?!)
  • "This is information, not instruction."
  • "Not my circus, not my monkeys."

Your New Relationship with Control

What you're building here:

  • Clear boundaries between your responsibility and everyone else's
  • Mental freedom from trying to manage unmanageable things
  • Emotional resilience that doesn't depend on perfect circumstances
  • Energy efficiency so you can show up fully for what actually matters

Practicing this is about becoming incredibly intentional with where you invest your precious mental and emotional energy. When you stop trying to control everything, you finally have the bandwidth to show up powerfully for the things that are actually yours.

Module 2
Exercises

Like physical therapy for your brain, these concepts need practice to become natural. That's why the exercises coming up work like training wheels for a new way of thinking.

Think of them like building your "Letting Go" strength:

1. Circle of Control Diagram
This tool helps you visually sort your current worries and concerns into clear categories: what you can control, what you can influence, and what you need to release completely. This exercise literally shows your brain the difference between productive concern and wasted energy.

2. Control Inventory Journal
This exercise takes you through specific situations where you felt frustrated or powerless and helps you identify concrete next steps you can actually take. Instead of staying stuck in "this shouldn't have happened," you'll move into "here's what I can do now."

3. Decision-Making Framework
This exercise gives you four key questions to filter any choice through, helping you make decisions from your values rather than your anxieties, building new mental pathways for clarity over chaos.

4. Energy Audit
This tool reveals exactly where your mental energy is going each week and helps you consciously redirect it toward what actually serves your peace and goals.

These exercises work together to retrain your brain's default response from "I need to control this" to "where can I best invest my energy?"

Quick Redirect Ideas

When you're trying to control the uncontrollable:

  • Take 5 deep breaths
  • Ask "Is this mine to control?"
  • Write it down, put it in a "not mine" box
  • Clean or organize one small thing
  • Journal about what you CAN do today
  • Text a friend about anything else
  • Ask "Will this matter in 5 years?"
  • Quick gratitude check (3 things)
  • Focus on something in your control circle

Finding what works is individual, so get creative! The goal is to catch yourself in the control spiral and redirect that energy somewhere useful.
